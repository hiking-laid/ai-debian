---
name: starbursting
description: Brainstorm an idea by generating questions instead of jumping to answers, using the six points of the star — Who, What, When, Where, Why, and How. Use when the user wants to explore a new idea, feature, or plan broadly before committing, or asks to "starburst" or brainstorm something.
---

Brainstorm by generating **questions**, not answers. The point of starbursting is to map the
whole territory of an idea before we commit to any part of it — good questions now prevent
expensive surprises later. Resist the urge to solve; first expand.

Put the idea at the centre of the star and work the six points. For each, generate a spread of
questions — go for breadth, including the awkward and obvious ones:

- **Who** — who is it for, who uses it, who maintains it, who is affected, who decides, who else
  has solved this?
- **What** — what is it exactly, what is in and out of scope, what could go wrong, what are the
  alternatives, what does success look like?
- **When** — when is it used, when does it run/trigger, when is it needed by, what is the
  sequence, what happens over time?
- **Where** — where does it live (system, file, surface), where does the data come from and go,
  where are the boundaries and integration points?
- **Why** — why do this at all, why now, why this way over another, why would it fail, why would
  someone not use it?
- **How** — how does it work, how is it built, how is it measured, how much effort/cost, how does
  it degrade or recover?

Rules of the exercise:

1. **Questions first, answers second.** Complete the full spread of questions across all six
   points before answering any of them. Do not collapse into a solution early.
2. **Aim for volume and range.** Several questions per point; quantity breeds quality. Include
   naive and uncomfortable questions — they surface hidden assumptions.
3. **Look up facts, don't invent them.** When a question can be answered from the environment
   (filesystem, code, tools, docs), find the real answer rather than guessing.
4. **Then answer the sharp ones.** After the map is drawn, answer the highest-leverage questions
   — the ones whose answers most change the shape of the idea — and flag which remain open.
5. **Hand back the open questions.** End by surfacing the unresolved, decision-worthy questions
   for me, and offer to go deeper (e.g. grill or steelman a specific branch).

The goal is a well-mapped idea with its unknowns made explicit, not a finished plan. The
decisions stay mine.
